After injury or surgery

Phantom limb pain

Phantom pain is felt in the amputated part that is no longer present. It differs from a painless phantom sensation.

Book a visit
On this pageWhat you may feelHow it is assessedApproaches to careWhen to seek urgent help
01

What you may feel

  • Burning or squeezing in the missing part
  • Episodic or continuous pain
02

How it is assessed

Assessment reviews attacks and the residual limb because local problems can coexist.

03

Approaches to care

Individual care may include pain management and selected rehabilitation methods such as mirror therapy. Response varies between people.

When to seek urgent help

Wound redness or discharge, fever, rapidly worsening pain or new weakness need prompt assessment. Sudden breathlessness or chest pain requires emergency care.
