Pain conditions / Hip, knee and foot

Hip, knee and foot

Patellar tendinopathy

Pain in the tendon below the kneecap often increases with jumping, running or rapid changes of direction.

Book a visit
On this pageWhat you may feelHow it is assessedApproaches to careWhen to seek urgent help
01

What you may feel

  • Local pain just below the kneecap at the tendon.
  • Jumping, running or stairs may aggravate the pain.
  • Squatting or loading the bent knee can be painful.
  • The tendon may feel stiff after sleep or rest.
  • Pain may ease during warm-up but return after exercise.
02

How it is assessed

Assessment considers tendon tenderness, knee strength and training volume while checking other joint causes.

Explain how far you can walk, which footwear you use and which movements hurt. Mention recent injury or changes in activity.

03

Approaches to care

Rehabilitation centres on load adjustment and staged strengthening before returning to jumping.

Discuss realistic walking, work or exercise goals with your clinician. At follow-up, describe changes after activity and how long pain lasted.

When to seek urgent help

Inability to bear weight after injury, a hot red joint with fever, or a suddenly cold pale foot need urgent assessment.
