Nerve pain

Diabetic neuropathic pain

Nerve damage associated with diabetes can cause pain, especially in both feet. Numbness also matters, even without pain.

On this pageWhat you may feelHow it is assessedApproaches to careWhen to seek urgent help
01

What you may feel

  • Burning or stabbing pain in the feet, often on both sides.
  • Pain may be more noticeable at night and disturb sleep.
  • Numbness, tingling or pain from light contact may occur.
  • Reduced temperature and pain sensation can let a wound go unnoticed.
  • Walking may feel unsteady or the feet may feel weak.
02

How it is assessed

Assessment checks foot skin, sensation and circulation, alongside diabetes management.

Identify the precise areas of pain and numbness and describe the effect of touch. Bring details of other conditions, medicines and previous procedures.

03

Approaches to care

Pain care is combined with diabetes management and daily foot care. A new ulcer, redness or signs of infection need prompt assessment.

Discuss how pain affects sleep, work and movement. At follow-up, report changes in sensation and strength as well as pain intensity.

When to seek urgent help

A new foot wound, redness, warmth or swelling needs prompt assessment, even with little pain. Deterioration with fever or a suddenly cold pale foot needs urgent help.
